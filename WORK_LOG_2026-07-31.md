## 1. Where the day started

I had two documents that had already passed a structural audit and a verifier loop. What I did not have was:

- Jeroen's review incorporated
- any confidence that the flow held up if you walked it as an actual guard
- any confidence that the writing did not read as machine-generated

That last one matters more than it sounds. These go to the Chief Product & Security Officer and the technical lead. A document that reads as generated invites the reader to discount the thinking behind it, whether or not the thinking is sound.

## 2. What I did, in order

### 2.1 Incorporated Jeroen's review

Five things came out of his feedback and all five ran through both documents:

| Feedback | What changed |
|---|---|
| Onboarding must set the device up, not just teach | Three setup gates: permissions, NFC, background tracking |
| Do not over-promise verification | Reframed from "SAM verifies the setting" to "SAM asks, and reading it back is the better target" |
| Links must not become an escape route | Links duplicated into the flow rather than pointing at the app's own settings screen |
| Tips belong in context | Tips from the train-the-trainer deck taught inside the practice steps, not read out as a list |
| It replaces part of the onboarding presentation | Stated explicitly in both documents |

Commit `7216837`.

### 2.2 Redesigned the visual style

The first design was rejected — the words used were "very uneasy and not pleasant to look at". I rebuilt it as a calm editorial layout: Georgia headings, one restrained accent colour, quiet status labels instead of filled pills, open tables with hairline rules instead of boxed grids.

> [!note] Why this is not cosmetic
> A document that is unpleasant to look at gets skimmed. Skimming a specification is how requirements get missed. The design is part of whether the content lands.

### 2.3 Aligned the flow with the real app

I supplied real screenshots of the app rather than working from assumption. Several claims in the spec did not survive contact with them:

- **"Asking a question creates no record" was false.** A spoken question had been logged as a Procedure activity. Changed to "nothing live results", and the requirement now says the practice context must suppress that record.
- The Location screen already reads permission state and offers a settings link, which is the evidence that reading settings back is achievable.
- Activities have live consequences today: supervisor alerts, KPI impact, items in the Pronect Action Tracker. Those are now the named surfaces the exclusion filter must cover.

### 2.4 Asked for a strict verifier on whether the writing sounds human

This was the pivot of the day. I ran a verifier whose primary gate was: does this read as written by a person, especially the step-by-step and the quoted lines SAM says to the guard.

It failed the documents. So did a second pass after the first round of fixes. What it found, with evidence:

- 44 em dashes in the brief, 78 in the companion
- negative parallelism ("X, not Y") used as a house formula at six section heads
- three setup prompts that were one sentence with the nouns swapped
- seven labels for one referent, eight for another
- long identical runs of explanatory prose copy-pasted between the two documents
- uniform sentence length

Measured before and after:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Em dashes (brief) | 44 | 2 |
| Em dashes (companion) | 78 | 7 |
| Mean sentence length (brief) | 23.4 words | 18.0 |
| Sentences under 8 words (brief) | 6 | 21 |

Commits `8a66d32`, `2cde9a3`.

### 2.5 Walked the flow as a guard

I ran a second, different check: role-play the flow literally as a 46-year-old guard on a first shift with a mid-range Android. This found things no prose review would.

> [!warning] The defect that mattered most
> **A step that could not be completed as written.** SAM told the guard to "add where it happened" — but the seeded incident already said "from the site office", while the acceptance criterion required the word "reception", which is never spoken to the guard. In a flow that cannot be skipped. The seed now carries no location and SAM names the change it wants.

Others:

- **SAM told a lie the flow contradicted.** "Nothing you say gets filed", four screens before "That's it written up" and a saved report on screen.
- **The permissions gate contradicted itself.** It both linked to the app's settings page and claimed not to. Split into two consistent terms: *the phone's settings page for SAM OnSite* and *the in-app settings screen*.
- **The requirement and its acceptance criterion disagreed.** FR-08 forbids writing the activity for the question step; AC-06 assumed it was written and merely flagged. QA signs off against the AC, so the build would have shipped the behaviour the requirement calls unsafe.
- **"No path to the home screen except by completing it"** was contradicted by three exits I had added. All three are now named in the requirement.
- **The progress record had nothing to resume onto** — no reference to the practice report or message, while a test explicitly covers resuming on a different device.

### 2.6 The risk that runs the other way

The specification was thorough about practice data leaking *into* live operations and silent about the reverse.

A guard is thirty seconds into being taught that this is how you report things. Something actually happens. They press Talk and report it — and the safety mechanism swallows it: flagged as training, hidden from the supervisor it should have reached. The refresher puts experienced guards back in the same position indefinitely.

This is now a requirement. It is the finding I would put in front of Vincent first.

### 2.7 Re-scoped to an MVP

I had added seven requirements in one pass because two reviewers asked for them. That is letting reviewers set scope. Corrected: split into what a first build must do and what is worth doing later, with the deferrals written down as decisions rather than left as omissions.

**Deferred, with reasons stated in the document:**

- Detecting that what the guard reported is not the practice incident — needs content comparison and a threshold nobody has defined. The same risk is covered far more cheaply by a banner on every step plus the exit that already exists.
- The runtime capability check that refuses to launch without the exclusion filter — the MVP never leaves DEV/UAT, so this is a go-live gate. Rescoping it also removed a lockout I had created, where it could refuse to start with no route to the home screen.

**Kept**, because each is a few lines and each prevents a dead end: microphone checked at first use, NFC skipped on phones without the chip, a retry floor, the practice banner, one plain sentence about what a supervisor can see.

Commit `99f5863`.

### 2.8 Made device readiness half the purpose

The last correction of the day, and the one that changed the most. The spec was treating the three setup gates as a preamble to the "real" training. That is wrong. Onboarding has **two outputs** and both must hold when it ends: the guard can use SAM, and the phone is ready to work.

Stating it that way forced three changes:

1. **Definition of done now includes the device.** A guard who finishes and then cannot scan a checkpoint has not been onboarded. Neither document said this before.
2. **Completion could not rest on the guard's word.** If "everything is ready" is the promise, a guard tapping *Done* on a gate they did not complete breaks it silently, and the failure surfaces weeks later mid-shift. So the permissions gate now **reads the state back**: a permission still off returns the guard to that gate with that permission named.
3. **The engineering question got sharper.** Not "could SAM check the settings?" but: *permission state is already readable — can NFC and background-tracking state be read the same way?* Until that is answered those two stay on the guard's word, and the residual risk is named in the error table rather than hidden.

The gates stay up front and mandatory. An earlier suggestion to move NFC and background tracking after the practice steps is dropped — setup completeness is the point, not a delay before the teaching.

Commit `92a10c5`.

## 4. What I got wrong today

Kept deliberately, because the corrections are the useful part of this log.

- **I let two reviewers set the scope.** Seven new requirements in one pass, with no MVP filter applied. Caught and corrected, but I should have applied the filter myself before the reviewers ever saw it.
- **My own batch edit introduced a broken sentence.** A find-and-replace pass across the source left a subjectless fragment in the flow. Automated edits to prose need the same verification as the prose itself.
- **I under-scoped device readiness.** I treated the setup gates as scaffolding around the training rather than half of what onboarding is for, and did not notice that the definition of done omitted the device entirely.
- **I marked two requirements as Confirmed while they rested on unconfirmed platform behaviour.** Both are now Check-with-engineering. This is the exact failure mode I set out to avoid on 29 July and it recurred.

## 5. How I used AI, specifically

Not "I used AI to write it". The split matters:

**AI did:**
- Generated the Word documents programmatically, so layout is reproducible from source rather than hand-formatted
- Ran adversarial verification — an independent strict verifier and a role-played guard walkthrough, neither of which shared context with the drafting
- Measured what a read-through cannot: em-dash density, sentence-length distribution, cross-document copy-paste, ID gaps in traceability
- Applied fixes across two documents consistently

**I did:**
- Set the quality bar, and refused output until it was met
- Made every scope call, including overruling the reviewers on MVP boundaries
- Supplied the real screenshots that falsified several claims in the draft
- Made the product judgement that device readiness is half the purpose of onboarding, which reshaped the completion model
- Decided what stays honest: which requirements are Confirmed, which are Proposed, which need engineering

> [!tip] The transferable bit
> The value was not in generating text. It was in **building a check I could not perform myself** — I cannot audit 31 requirements for internal consistency by reading, and a role-play walkthrough found a step that literally could not be completed. Then applying judgement to what the check returned, rather than implementing every finding.
